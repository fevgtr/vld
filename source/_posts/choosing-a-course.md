**Week 1 · 4 September 2026 · Data Structures (数据结构), Tsinghua University · XuetangX**

This session was deciding what my VLD actually is. The direction was clear — data structures and algorithms, in C++ — but choosing the right course turned out to be a real decision.

<!-- more -->

## 1. What I worked on

Comparing four candidate courses and committing to one: Tsinghua University's *Data Structures* (数据结构), taught by Prof. Deng Junhui, free on XuetangX. I also read through the prerequisites and found the online judge where programming assignments get submitted.

## 2. What was challenging, and how I handled it

The hard part was that every obvious option had a catch, and I couldn't tell which one mattered until I worked out what I actually needed.

- **Stanford CS106B** — everything's public and it's in C++, but nothing I submit gets marked.
- **UC San Diego's specialisation** on Coursera — properly graded, but it needs a financial-aid application, and it mixes Python, Java and C++.
- **UIUC Accelerated CS Fundamentals** — C++, free to audit, closest to a drop-in answer. But about 60 hours total, which is thin for a whole year.
- **Tsinghua's 数据结构** — free, entirely C++, and it has a real online judge with graded assignments.

I wrote down the three things I actually needed — work that gets graded, C++ throughout, and no cost — and only one option had all three. That's how I got unstuck: the comparison was impossible until I'd decided what I was comparing on.

I also considered skipping a formal course and just grinding problems on USACO Guide and CSES, since I have a competitive programming background. But I decided against it: problem sets teach you to recognise patterns you've already seen, while a structured course builds the foundations I want to strengthen — things like templates, virtual methods, and formal algorithm analysis.

## 3. How well I ran my own learning

**Self-score: 89**

I made a real decision and I can defend it. I set my own criteria, evaluated four options against them, and committed to one with a clear rationale. The reason it's 89 and not 100 is that listing the criteria should have been my starting point instead of something I arrived at after browsing course pages for a while — once I had the criteria, the decision was fast.

## 4. What I'll do differently next session

Send Mr. Wright the course link and my one-sentence reason before I do anything else, then enrol and register on the judge in the same session — registration needs to use the same email as the XuetangX account or the grades don't sync.
